Efficiency Scorecard

The rework made each answer 140× cheaper and cut a council member's reload from 291 addon messages to 124. The run-recovery cache then cut that reload to 2.

Two changes are measured here against the addon as it stood before them. The Full Comms and Efficiency Rework ran from 0.40.8 to 0.41.13. The run-recovery cache is 0.41.14, measured on its own against the addon after the rework and against the addon before it. Every size was measured in the game client, and every CPU figure in the game. Times come from a model of the game's message limits unless marked as measured in game.

Part two · 0.41.14

The run-recovery cache, against both

After the rework, a reload was the one thing still sending the run around again: a council member's recovery still moved 124 messages through the raid, and a raider's 7. The cache keeps each player's own copy of the run at logout. After a reload, the loot master checks a fingerprint of each item and sends only what changed. When nothing has, that is one small message.

How it got here

0.40.8 – 0.40.12 · measuring

Instruments first

/slc perf, /slc stress and /slc comms, and startup moved out of the game's shared loading hooks so the game's profiler charges SLC's time to SLC. Every figure on this page comes from these tools.

0.40.13 · roster

One roster service

Roster changes are coalesced into one diff instead of a reaction to every raw roster event, and presence comes from the client that joins.

0.41.0 · transport

A new wire format and three lanes

A probe showed the addon channel carries every byte unchanged, so the text encoding gave way to raw bytes in messages of up to 255 bytes. Traffic runs in three lanes (control, bulk, presence) under one byte budget.

A quarter fewer bytes and a third fewer messages; awards no longer wait behind bulk traffic

0.41.1 · painting

The redraw scheduler

The council window paints after events, about once every 0.15 s at most, and updates rows in place instead of rebuilding the table on every answer.

The window's share of a 30 × 12 stress fell from 1,423 ms to 49 ms

0.41.3 – 0.41.4 · answers and recovery traffic

Batching, a logout save and quieter recovery

A raider's answers leave as one batch. The loot master's copy of the run is built once at logout instead of on every message. Recovery asks the loot master directly, "no run" is said once, and messages that arrive before the run are held and replayed.

The cost of one answer stopped growing with the run

0.41.5 – 0.41.9 · the remaining windows

Everything on the scheduler

Council, bags and history moved onto the roster service, bags travel as item strings, and every remaining window paints through the scheduler. The response window ignores other raiders' answers entirely.

An answer costs about 0.04 ms, and no stress frame passes 16.7 ms

0.41.11 · recovery in parts

A council member's recovery in parts

The loot master sends a small skeleton, and every raider sends their own rows, so no single sender carries the whole run.

12 messages from the loot master instead of 66; seconds instead of about a minute

0.41.12 – 0.41.13 · the rework's last fixes

Correct labels and a safer rebuild

The sweep carries the loot master's verdict, so every client labels silent raiders the same way. A council member reloading while the loot master rebuilds waits for the rebuild instead of getting half a run.

0.41.14 · the run-recovery cache

Every player keeps a copy

Each player saves the run at logout. After a reload the loot master checks each item's fingerprint and sends only what changed, replacing anything out of date. The copy also puts back the player's own answers and votes if the run came back without them, and a run that ended while they were away stays ended.

A quiet reload: 2 messages instead of 124 for a council member, 1 instead of 7 for a raider

Where it stops

What the cache does not change

  • Anything outside a reload: answers, votes and painting cost what the rework left them at.
  • A council member who reloads while answers are still pouring in: most items have changed, so the run comes in parts, as after the rework.
  • A crash writes no saved variables, so there is no copy to use.
  • A player who joins mid-run has no copy either.

Sizes: the game client's own encoder, through /slc snapshotsize (0.34.78 for the older text encoding; 0.41.10 to 0.41.14 since). CPU: /slc stress's own stopwatch on 0.40.11 and on 0.41.8, the last stress baseline; nothing after 0.41.8 added work per answer. Times: a model of the game's per-prefix limit (10 messages at once, then one a second), with SLC's byte governor from 0.41.0 on (4,000 B, then 800 B/s), plus 0.3 s of latency.

Before 0.41.4, a council member's three asks, two seconds apart, were each answered with the whole run, because the first answer took over a minute to arrive. The cache was checked in game on three clients with 6-item runs.
